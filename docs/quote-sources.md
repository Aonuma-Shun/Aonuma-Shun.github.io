# Fragment Sources

Reviewed on 2026-09-23. The ten entries below follow the order of `quotes` in
`src/App.tsx`. Selection favors short, non-spoiling passages that remain welcoming
outside their story context. Work links in the UI remain Moegirl introductions;
they are not the evidence used for attribution.

Unless stated otherwise, the website's Chinese and English wording is a local
translation of the identified Japanese passage, not a claimed official
localization. Some entries are promotional copy or an interface prompt rather
than dialogue. Condensed passages are explicitly identified below.

## 1. Umineko: Without Love

- Passage: the recurring maxim about love and perception. Existing bilingual
  wording retained.
- Evidence: [Crunchyroll's discussion of Umineko](https://www.crunchyroll.com/hi/news/features/2020/10/4/opinion-how-umineko-changed-my-entire-approach-to-fictional-media)
  quotes the English wording directly. This is editorial corroboration, not
  proof of a particular licensed game's English script.

## 2. Umineko: The Single Element

- Context: Episode 2, Beatrice speaking to Shannon about the elements composing
  the world, then identifying love as the single element.
- Evidence: [Umineko Project's Japanese EP2 script, `umi2_1.txt`](https://github.com/umineko-project/umineko-scripting/blob/master/story/ep2/jp/umi2_1.txt).
  This is a community-maintained script, not a publisher-hosted translation.
- Locate `一なる元素` and the subsequent answer identifying love.
- Translation: a condensed rendering of that connected exchange, not a claim
  that the new one-sentence wording is a verbatim Japanese line. The passage
  contrasts many elements with one; it does not number love as the first element.

## 3. Ever17: Archimedes' Principle

- Context: Takeshi's question in Tsugumi's route, near its ending.
- Evidence: [2006 route review](https://himicomu.blog34.fc2.com/blog-entry-448.html)
  and [2009 route discussion](https://kichimagro.exblog.jp/8823436/) independently
  record the question in Japanese. These are contemporary player records, not
  an official script; attribution is corroborated, but a licensed English
  localization has not been verified.
- Translation note: the question asks whether the listener knows the principle,
  rather than asking them to remember it.

## 4. Higurashi: Five Minutes

- Context: Rika, Minagoroshi chapter, after Keiichi changes the course of the
  club's game. The five-minute wording is present in the script, not added as
  motivational copy.
- Evidence: [07th-Mod's bilingual script, `_mina_002_1.txt`](https://github.com/07th-mod/minagoroshi/blob/master/Update/_mina_002_1.txt).
  Locate voice cue `ps3/s12/05/180500162`.
- Translation: local rendering of the Japanese line. The community patch also
  provides English text, but this review does not certify it as an unmodified
  official localization.

## 5. Steins;Gate: An Unknown Future

- Context: Okabe's reflection on an unknown future and its possibilities.
- Evidence: [licensed tenth-anniversary Anique collection](https://lp.anique.jp/cp/steinsgate01/),
  the artwork entry dated 2012-05-24. The page identifies the rights holders and
  states that the service is licensed.
- Translation: condensed excerpt. The intervening reference to the characters'
  reunion is omitted in both languages so the fragment stands alone; the
  causal connection between uncertainty and possibilities is retained.

## 6. Wonderful Everyday: Live Happily

- Evidence: [transcription and translation of the Official Visual Archive](https://amenowoto.blog/blog/2026-02-03/subahibi-archive-translation/),
  including SCA-Ji's commentary on page 099 and the interview; corroborated by
  the [2010 ending notes](https://w.atwiki.jp/suba/pages/72.html).
- Only the short imperative is retained. The previously appended conclusion
  was not established as part of this phrase.
- Attribution is to its use in the game, not a claim that the game originated
  the philosophical expression. The archive is accessed through a fan-hosted
  transcription, not the original printed volume.

## 7. Rewrite: Rewriting Fate

- Kind: official story/promotion copy, not attributed to a speaking character.
- Evidence: [Key's story page](https://key.visualarts.gr.jp/rewrite/story.html)
  (the text is in an image), also reproduced in a
  [2016 release announcement](https://www.famitsu.com/news/201609/15115674.html).
- Translation: a compact question retaining the original reference to her fate.

## 8. Little Busters!: The World's Secret

- Kind: the opening question in Little Busters! EX asking whether the player
  already knows the world's secret. No character attribution is asserted.
- Evidence: [EX FAQ](https://w.atwiki.jp/littlebus/pages/46.html) and an independent
  [2008 first-play account](https://do-gugan.com/~furuta/archives/2008/10/post_260.html)
  both record the actual opening prompt and explain its yes/no choices.
- Existing bilingual wording retained. These are corroborating player records,
  not proof of a particular licensed English edition's exact wording.

## 9. CLANNAD: A Beautiful World

- Context: Kotomi's route; a passage in her parents' letter, read by Kotomi.
- Evidence: [Japanese script and fan-translation working text, `SEEN4513`](https://www.baka-tsuki.org/project/index.php?title=Clannad_VN:SEEN4513),
  entries 0404-0405.
- Translation: two consecutive clauses combined into one sentence in both
  languages. The source is a community transcript, not the official English
  release. The scene also appears in the anime, but the game passage is the
  attribution used here.

## 10. AIR: Summer

- Kind: an excerpt from the official story synopsis, not character dialogue.
- Evidence: [Prototype's AIR product page](https://www.prot.co.jp/sp/rakuten/appli/air/index.php),
  the closing passage of the synopsis.
- Translation: only the first sentence about summer continuing is retained and
  newly translated. The former English sentence was not natural English and
  was not verified as an official localization.

## Removed Entries

- Remember11 and CROSS CHANNEL: the exact previous wording was not established
  confidently enough during this review. Removal does not prove it is false.
- Muv-Luv Alternative: the shouted wording felt too forceful for this homepage.
- The House in Fata Morgana: the fragment can sound dismissive of others'
  suffering without its narrative context.
- Fate/stay night and Full Metal Daemon Muramasa: omitted to keep the selection
  small and less dependent on heroic-story framing; not classified as false.

Do not add future entries merely because a quote aggregator labels them with a
work's name. Record a scene, script location, official publication, or multiple
specific independent records, and distinguish translation from exact quotation.
